Cost of Poor Quality (COPQ) Quantifier

Quantify the full cost of poor quality using the Prevention–Appraisal–Failure (PAF) model. Enter costs by category and receive a total COPQ with category breakdown, industry benchmark comparison, prioritized reduction opportunities, and a management-review-ready summary. Connects to AS9100D 9.1.3 / ISO 9001 9.1.3 analysis of QMS performance and cost of poor quality reporting.

Enter costs in your local currency. The tool is currency-agnostic — all outputs are in the same unit you enter. Blank fields are treated as zero. Annualize all values (use the same time period throughout).

Prevention Costs — Investment to prevent defects from occurring

Appraisal Costs — Cost of measuring and monitoring quality

Internal Failure Costs — Defects found before delivery to customer

External Failure Costs — Defects found after delivery to customer

Reference: PAF Model & Industry Benchmarks

Prevention + Appraisal = "Cost of Conformance" — money spent to ensure quality.

Internal + External Failure = "Cost of Non-Conformance (CONC)" — money lost to poor quality.

Typical COPQ benchmarks (as % of Revenue):

  • World-class organizations: 1–3% of revenue
  • Average manufacturers: 5–15% of revenue
  • Poor performers: 20–40% of revenue
  • Aerospace/defense (typical): 3–8% of revenue
  • Medical devices (typical): 4–10% of revenue
  • Automotive Tier 1 (typical): 2–6% of revenue

Healthy Prevention:Appraisal:Failure ratio target: 30–40% : 30–40% : 20–30%

Failure-heavy organizations (Failure > 50% of COPQ) should shift investment to Prevention — every $1 invested in Prevention typically eliminates $4–$10 in Failure costs.

AS9100D / ISO 9001 connection: COPQ analysis supports clause 9.1.3 (analysis and evaluation) and clause 9.3 (management review — input: quality performance and trend data). It also directly feeds corrective action prioritization (clause 10.2) and continual improvement (clause 10.3).

Reference: Prevention-Appraisal-Failure (PAF) cost of quality model (Feigenbaum, Juran). Clause references: AS9100D / ISO 9001:2015 clauses 9.1.3 and 9.3 by intent only. Benchmark ranges are industry approximations — actual performance varies by sector, product complexity, and maturity.
