Process 1 — Management (MG)

Leadership commitment, quality policy, objectives, management review, and risk-based thinking across the QMS.

⚡ Key MDSAP grading note: Management process findings are weighted heavily. Grade 5 findings here trigger immediate authority notification across all 5 participating nations.

1.1 Quality Management System Foundation

—
Quality Manual (or equivalent documented scope/QMS framework) exists, is current, and defines the scope of the QMS including exclusions with justification.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Quality Policy is established, communicated to all personnel, reviewed for continuing suitability, and available as documented information.
All 5 Authorities
Quality Objectives are established at relevant functions and levels, are measurable, monitored, communicated, and updated as appropriate.
All 5 Authorities
Processes needed for the QMS are identified, their sequence and interaction are determined, and inputs/outputs of each process are defined.
All 5 Authorities

1.2 Leadership & Organizational Roles

—
Top management demonstrates leadership and commitment to the QMS (e.g., resource allocation, promoting customer focus, integration with business strategy).
All 5 Authorities
Management Representative (or equivalent role) is designated with authority and responsibility for ensuring QMS requirements are met and reporting to top management.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Organizational roles, responsibilities, and authorities are defined, documented, and communicated throughout the organization.
All 5 Authorities

1.3 Management Review

—
Management Review is conducted at planned intervals (minimum annually) with records maintained.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Management Review inputs include: audit results, customer feedback, process/product performance, CAPA status, risk assessment, regulatory changes, and previous review actions.
All 5 Authorities
Management Review outputs include decisions and actions related to QMS improvements, resource needs, and device/process changes.
All 5 Authorities

1.4 Risk Management (QMS Level)

—
Risk-based thinking is applied to QMS planning (risks and opportunities identified and addressed at the QMS level, not just device-level).
All 5 Authorities
ISO 14971 risk management process is implemented for product/process risk and is linked to the QMS (risk management file exists per device family).
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
ℹ️ This tool is a self-assessment aid. It is not a substitute for a formal MDSAP audit by an Authorized Auditing Organization (AAO). Regulatory requirements are subject to change; verify against current MDSAP Audit Approach documents.

Process 2 — Device Marketing Authorization & Facility Registration (DMAFR)

Ensures devices have valid marketing authorizations in each market and the facility is properly registered with applicable regulatory authorities.

⚡ This process has the highest authority-specific variation. Requirements differ significantly by country — check each authority column carefully.

2.1 Marketing Authorizations — United States (FDA)

—
All US-marketed devices have valid FDA clearance/approval (510(k), PMA, De Novo, or HDE) or are exempt; FDA Establishment Registration is current (annual renewal).
FDACritical
Device Listing is current and accurate in FDA's database (21 CFR Part 807). All listed devices reflect current labeling and indications.
FDA
UDI labels applied to device packaging (DI on all levels; PI on primary package where required); GUDID database submissions are current per FDA UDI Rule.
FDA

2.2 Marketing Authorizations — Canada (Health Canada)

—
Medical Device Licence (MDL) is current for all Class II/III/IV devices marketed in Canada; Class I devices have Establishment Licence only.
Health CanadaCritical
Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL) is current and reflects current activities (manufacture, import, distribute).
Health Canada

2.3 Marketing Authorizations — Brazil (ANVISA), Australia (TGA), Japan (PMDA)

—
ANVISA Registro (product registration) is current for all devices marketed in Brazil; GMP Certificate from MDSAP AAO is accepted by ANVISA in lieu of separate ANVISA inspection.
ANVISA
TGA ARTG listing/registration is current for all devices marketed in Australia; TGA accepts MDSAP audit report in lieu of separate TGA audit (verify TGA's current MDSAP recognition scope).
TGA
PMDA marketing authorization (Shonin/Ninsho) is current for all devices marketed in Japan; GMP/QMS certification from Japanese registrar (or MDSAP-equivalent accepted by PMDA) is current.
PMDA
A process exists to identify and track pending regulatory changes/new requirements across all applicable authorities; responsibility is assigned.
All 5 Authorities

Process 3 — Design & Development (DD)

Controls over the design and development of medical devices to ensure requirements are met and devices are safe and effective.

⚡ FDA requires a Design History File (DHF) — auditors will sample DHF entries against design output records and verify traceability. Health Canada, ANVISA, TGA, and PMDA require Technical Documentation maintained per regional requirements.

3.1 Design Planning & Controls

—
Design and development planning is documented for each device/project; plans identify stages, review points, responsibilities, and required resources.
All 5 Authorities
Design inputs are defined, documented, and reviewed for adequacy (functional, performance, safety, regulatory, and usability requirements).
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Design outputs are documented in a form that allows verification against design inputs; outputs include device specifications, drawings, software code, labeling, and manufacturing procedures.
All 5 Authorities

3.2 Design Review, Verification & Validation

—
Design reviews are conducted at appropriate stages; reviewers include personnel not directly responsible for the design stage; results and actions are recorded.
All 5 Authorities
Design verification confirms outputs meet inputs; verification results, methods, and acceptance criteria are documented.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Design validation confirms device meets user needs and intended use (including clinical evaluation, usability/HFE studies, and simulated or real-use testing).
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Design transfer to production is documented; manufacturing process capabilities are verified before final release.
All 5 Authorities
Design changes are identified, reviewed, verified/validated as appropriate, approved, and communicated before implementation; impact on regulatory submissions assessed.
All 5 Authorities

3.3 Design History File / Technical Documentation

—
Design History File (DHF) maintained for each device type; DHF demonstrates the device was developed in accordance with the approved design plan. (FDA: 21 CFR 820.30(j))
FDACritical
Technical Documentation (technical file) is maintained and kept current; includes device description, design specifications, risk management file, clinical data summary, and labeling.
Health CanadaANVISATGAPMDA

Process 4 — Purchasing & Supplier Controls (PC)

Controls to ensure purchased products and services conform to requirements, with supplier evaluation and monitoring proportionate to risk.

⚡ MDSAP auditors specifically look for risk-based supplier classification and evidence of actual re-evaluation — not just a list of approved suppliers. Critical suppliers require documented performance reviews.

4.1 Supplier Qualification & Approved Supplier List

—
Approved Supplier List (ASL) is maintained; suppliers are classified by risk/criticality; qualification criteria are defined per risk tier.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Supplier qualification records exist for critical/high-risk suppliers (surveys, on-site audits, certificates, quality agreements as appropriate).
All 5 Authorities
Suppliers are periodically re-evaluated (frequency commensurate with risk); re-evaluation records are maintained and inform continued approval status.
All 5 Authorities

4.2 Purchasing Controls & Incoming Inspection

—
Purchase orders/specifications clearly define requirements for products and services (quality requirements, standards references, documentation requirements).
All 5 Authorities
Incoming inspection or equivalent verification activities are conducted per defined procedures; records are maintained showing results and release authority.
All 5 Authorities
Nonconforming purchased product is controlled; supplier notification, disposition, and SCAR/corrective action process is in place.
All 5 Authorities

Process 5 — Production & Service Controls (PSC)

Controls over production and service provision to ensure devices are manufactured consistently and meet specifications.

⚡ Device History Records (DHRs) are frequently the focus of FDA inspection sampling. Auditors will pull specific DHRs and trace them against the Device Master Record (DMR). Ensure your DHRs are complete and retrievable by lot/serial.

5.1 Production Process Controls

—
Device Master Record (DMR) / Technical File exists for each device type; includes device specifications, production procedures, quality specifications, labeling, and packaging specifications.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Device History Record (DHR) is maintained for each manufactured lot/batch/unit; DHR demonstrates the device was manufactured in accordance with the DMR.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Equipment used in production/quality is identified, maintained, calibrated (where applicable), and records maintained; out-of-tolerance equipment impact is assessed.
All 5 Authorities
Special processes (sterilization, coating, welding, etc.) are validated before implementation; revalidation triggers are defined and followed.
All 5 Authorities
Software used in production or quality systems is validated for intended use (IQ/OQ/PQ or equivalent) per a defined validation plan.
All 5 Authorities

5.2 Labeling, Packaging & UDI

—
Labeling control procedure is in place; labels are reviewed and approved before use; label inspection is part of the production release process.
All 5 Authorities
UDI requirements are met: DI applied to primary/secondary/tertiary packaging levels as required; GUDID (FDA), UDI-DI (HC/ANVISA/TGA) submissions are current and accurate.
FDAHealth CanadaANVISATGA

5.3 Complaint Handling & Post-Market

—
Complaint handling procedure exists; all complaints are received, evaluated, investigated (where warranted), and dispositioned with records maintained.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
MDR/Vigilance reporting process is documented; staff know when to escalate a complaint to reportable event status; reporting timelines per authority are defined.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Post-Market Surveillance (PMS) system is implemented; PMS data feeds back into risk management and design/production controls.
All 5 Authorities

Process 6 — Measurement, Analysis & Improvement (MAI)

Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and improvement activities to ensure conformity of devices and QMS, and to facilitate continual improvement.

⚡ MDSAP auditors follow NC trails rigorously — they will sample CAPA records, check escape points, verify effectiveness verification was done, and look for systemic patterns across product families. Weak CAPA is the most common Grade 3+ finding.

6.1 Internal Audit Program

—
Internal audit program covers all QMS processes and all applicable regulatory requirements on a defined schedule; frequency is risk-based.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Auditors are independent of the areas they audit; auditor competence is documented (training records, lead auditor qualifications).
All 5 Authorities
Audit findings are communicated to management; CAPAs are opened for nonconformities; effectiveness of CAPAs is verified before closure.
All 5 Authorities

6.2 CAPA — Corrective & Preventive Action

—
CAPA procedure is documented; it requires root cause analysis (not just symptom treatment) and includes containment, root cause, corrective action, preventive action, and verification of effectiveness.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
CAPA records are maintained; open CAPAs are tracked with due dates and owner assignments; aging/overdue CAPAs are escalated per defined process.
All 5 Authorities
CAPA actions are evaluated for potential impact on safety/performance; regulatory authority notification assessed where applicable.
All 5 Authorities

6.3 Nonconforming Product/Output

—
Nonconforming product is identified, segregated, and controlled to prevent unintended use; disposition decisions (rework, use-as-is with justification, scrap) are documented and authorized.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
If a nonconforming device is released (e.g., waiver/concession), the process for obtaining authorization is documented; patient safety risk is formally assessed.
All 5 Authorities

6.4 Recalls, Field Corrections & Vigilance

—
Corrections and removals (recall) procedure is documented; process covers detection, risk assessment, regulatory notification, field action execution, and effectiveness check.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
Regulatory reporting timelines for adverse events/MDRs are documented and known by responsible staff (FDA: 5/10/30 days; HC: 10/30 days; ANVISA: 72 hrs/15 days; TGA: 2/10/30 days; PMDA: 15/30 days).
FDAHealth CanadaANVISATGAPMDA

Process 7 — Supporting Processes (SP)

Document and record control, training, infrastructure, and competence management that underpin all other QMS processes.

⚡ Supporting process failures cascade into every other process finding. Auditors sample document control across all processes — a missing record or an unapproved document can generate findings in every process it touches.

7.1 Document Control

—
Document control procedure ensures documents are reviewed, approved prior to issue, current version is available at point of use, and obsolete documents are prevented from unintended use.
All 5 AuthoritiesCritical
External documents (regulatory standards, customer specs, supplier documents) are identified, controlled, and updated when the external source changes.
All 5 Authorities
Electronic document management system (if used) is validated for intended use and access controls are enforced.
All 5 Authorities

7.2 Record Control

—
Record retention schedule is defined; records are legible, identifiable, and retrievable; retention periods meet or exceed regulatory minimums (FDA: lifetime of device + 2 years minimum; HC: life of device; ANVISA: 5–15 yrs; TGA: 5 yrs minimum; PMDA: per device class).
All 5 Authorities
Electronic records (if used) meet regulatory data integrity requirements (21 CFR Part 11 for FDA; equivalent for other authorities); audit trails are enabled and reviewed.
FDAHealth Canada

7.3 Human Resources, Competence & Training

—
Competence requirements are defined for all roles affecting product quality and regulatory compliance; training needs are identified and training is provided.
All 5 Authorities
Training records are maintained and demonstrate effectiveness (competency assessment, OJT sign-off, exam results); records are retrievable per personnel.
All 5 Authorities
New employees and contractors performing QMS-regulated activities are trained before performing those activities independently.
All 5 Authorities

7.4 Infrastructure & Work Environment

—
Infrastructure requirements (buildings, equipment, IT) are identified and maintained; preventive maintenance program is in place and records are maintained.
All 5 Authorities
Work environment requirements (cleanliness, ESD, temperature, humidity, etc.) are defined, controlled, and monitored where they affect product quality; records are maintained.
All 5 Authorities
