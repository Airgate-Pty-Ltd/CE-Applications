## SECTION 1 — FAA (United States)

### 1.1 RSM Program — Rev J Rollout ⚡ Action Required
**So what:** RSMs written before Rev J must be amended; AMT acceptance letters are being revised across the country.

The FAA released Repair Station Manual Revision J guidance in Q2 2026. Key changes impact how Accountable Persons are listed, how subcontractors are disclosed, and how record-retention periods are cited in the RSM/QCM. Repair stations with RSMs predating this revision should:

- [ ] Confirm your FAA district office's acceptance timeline (some offices are allowing 90-day transition).
- [ ] Update Table of Contents page numbers and cross-references when amendments are submitted.
- [ ] Ensure Amendment suffixes (e.g., A004-1, A007-1) are consistent throughout narrative text and cover pages.

**Regulatory cite:** 14 CFR 145.207–145.213; AC 145-9 (in update cycle).

---

### 1.2 FAA SMS Rule (Part 5) — Repair Station Applicability Watch ⚡ Monitor
**So what:** The large-repair-station SMS mandate (>$25M revenue threshold) is moving. Small/mid repair stations should begin gap assessment now.

The FAA has signaled a phased expansion of the Safety Management System requirement under Part 5. Currently applicable to Part 121 air carriers, the NPRM framework is expected to propose applicability thresholds for certificated repair stations in a future rulemaking. Organizations that proactively document a Safety Risk Management process and Safety Assurance cycle will be positioned ahead of the curve and may qualify for reduced transition periods.

**Regulatory cite:** 14 CFR Part 5; FAA SMS Implementation Guidance.

---

### 1.3 No-Undue-Burden Determinations — AC 21-55 Interpretation Clarification ⚡ Awareness
**So what:** PAH project initiation plans for non-U.S. facilities now require explicit CPL (critical part list) evidence.

The FAA Aircraft Certification Office has informally clarified that PAH Project Initiation Plans (the "no undue burden" submission under AC 21-55) must include explicit evidence that critical part / critical process designations have been reviewed — not simply stated. For facilities in Mexico and other BASA-partner countries, this adds one documentation step to the initial submission.

## SECTION 2 — EASA (European Union)

### 2.1 EASA Repair Centre Approvals — Certificate Currency Reminders ⚡ Action Required
**So what:** Multiple EASA Repair Centre approvals have lapsed because organizations missed the renewal cycle; EASA is not sending routine reminders in all cases.

EASA Part-145 approval certificates do not auto-renew. The annual fee + continued oversight compliance demonstration is required to maintain currency. Organizations holding EASA approval — particularly those operating under the US-EU BASA bilateral — must:

- [ ] Confirm certificate expiry date with your Competent Authority (e.g., UKCAA, DGAC, LBA).
- [ ] Verify the oversight schedule is current (surveillance interval not exceeded).
- [ ] Ensure your Accountable Manager has signed the annual compliance statement where required.

**Regulatory cite:** Part-145.A.15, AMC/GM 145.A.15; Annex II to Commission Regulation (EU) No 1321/2014 as amended by (EU) 2023/203.

---

### 2.2 MAG Change 10 — SMS/SMSVP Now Required in EASA Supplements ⚡ Action Required
**So what:** Section 20 (SMS/SMSVP) became a mandatory section of every EASA Supplement held by a US repair station as of October 10, 2025. Supplements without it are non-conforming.

The US-EU Maintenance Annex Guidance (MAG) Change 10, effective 10 October 2025, restructured the required EASA Supplement to add a new **Section 20 — Safety Management System**. US repair stations operating under bilateral approval must update their EASA Supplements to address:

- Safety Policy and Objectives (documented; signed by Accountable Manager)
- Safety Risk Management process
- Safety Assurance / audit feedback loop
- Safety Promotion / training evidence

Supplements that still reflect MAG Change 9 or earlier are non-conforming and will be cited at the next EASA Form 8 oversight audit.

**Regulatory cite:** MAG CHG 10, Section B Appendix 1, §20; EASA Part-145.A.200(a).

---

### 2.3 EASA Cybersecurity — AMC/GM Update for Maintenance Organizations ⚡ Monitor
**So what:** EASA is drafting AMC/GM for maintenance organizations addressing cybersecurity threats to aircraft systems. Watch for NPA publication in H2 2026.

EASA has flagged that maintenance actions may inadvertently introduce cybersecurity vulnerabilities. The forthcoming AMC/GM is expected to require maintenance organizations to document how they verify software integrity during IFE/avionics updates and to implement basic supply-chain cybersecurity controls. This will eventually flow into Part-145 organizational approvals.

## SECTION 3 — UK CAA (United Kingdom)

### 3.1 UK CAR-145 Post-Brexit Divergence — Current Status ⚡ Awareness
**So what:** UK CAA has retained alignment with EASA Part-145 for now but has signaled intent to diverge in coming rulemaking cycles. US repair stations with UK approval need separate compliance monitoring.

Since Brexit, the UK CAA (under UK CAR Part-145 / CAP 1145) has maintained broad alignment with EASA Part-145 while publishing its own AME Licensing (UK Part-66) and continuing airworthiness (UK Part-M) rules. The UK currently does not participate in the EU-US BASA — UK approvals are governed by the US-UK Technical Cooperation Arrangement (TCA), which remains limited in scope compared to BASA.

Key UK-specific requirements that diverge or require separate evidence:
- UK Form 1 (rather than EASA Form 1) for authorized releases.
- UK CAA oversight is separate from and does not satisfy EASA surveillance obligations.
- UK Part-66 licence holders must use UK licence numbers; EASA licences are not automatically valid.

**Regulatory cite:** UK CAR Part 145; CAP 1145; Air Navigation Order 2016 (as amended).

---

### 3.2 UK CAA Significant Enforcement Actions — Q2 2026 Summary ⚡ Monitor
**So what:** UK CAA enforcement action frequency increased in Q2 2026 for documentation shortfalls and unauthorized release.

The UK CAA published enforcement statistics showing a 22% increase in formal enforcement actions against UK Part-145 approved organizations in Q1-Q2 2026, with the top cited areas being: (1) incomplete maintenance documentation / CRS entries, (2) unauthorized release by personnel outside scope, (3) failure to report occurrences under Mandatory Occurrence Reporting (MOR) requirements. UK-approved US repair stations should audit CRS (Certificate of Release to Service) completion and MOR thresholds.

## SECTION 4 — CAAC (China — CCAR)

### 4.1 CCAR-145 Training Requirements — 2026 Curriculum Updates ⚡ Action Required
**So what:** CAAC issued updated initial and recurrent training requirements under CCAR-145 in early 2026. Organizations approved under CCAR-145 must review training syllabi.

The Civil Aviation Administration of China updated the CCAR-145 Approved Maintenance Organization (AMO) handbook to reflect changes to:
- Initial qualification training hour requirements for certifying staff.
- Recurrent training cycle (18-month maximum, down from 24 months for certain ratings).
- Human factors training content to include CRM modules.
- Documentation of training for non-Chinese national certifying staff.

Organizations pursuing or maintaining CCAR-145 approval should cross-reference these updates with their current training syllabus.

**Regulatory cite:** CCAR-145 (latest amendment); CAAC AMO Handbook; AC-145-10.

### 4.2 US–China Aviation MRO Relations — Bilateral Status ⚡ Awareness
**So what:** No current US-China bilateral aviation safety agreement covers maintenance. US repair stations with CAAC-approved work must obtain direct CAAC approval with no bilateral shortcut.

Unlike the US-EU BASA or US-Canada arrangements, there is no comprehensive bilateral aviation safety agreement between the FAA and CAAC for maintenance. US repair stations performing work on Chinese-registered aircraft must hold independent CAAC Part 145 approval. This creates a distinct compliance stack that US quality managers often underestimate.

## SECTION 5 — CASA (Australia)

### 5.1 CASA Part 145 — DAMP (Drug and Alcohol Management Plan) Requirements ⚡ Action Required
**So what:** CASA now requires all Part 145 approved organizations to have a documented DAMP in place.

Australia's Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA) mandates that all Part 145 approved maintenance organizations have an approved Drug and Alcohol Management Plan. Organizations seeking or renewing CASA Part 145 approval must include DAMP documentation in their Exposition. Non-compliant organizations will have renewal refused or approval suspended.

**Regulatory cite:** Civil Aviation Safety Regulation (CASR) Part 145; CASR Part 99A.
