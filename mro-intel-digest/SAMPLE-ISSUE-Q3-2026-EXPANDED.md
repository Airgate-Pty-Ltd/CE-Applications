## 1. FAA UPDATES (Part 145 Repair Stations)

### 1.1 NEW AD: Electrical System Aging Aircraft (Boeing 737, 777; Airbus A320)
**Type:** Airworthiness Directive (AD 2026-12-01)  
**Effective Date:** September 15, 2026  
**Applicability:** All Boeing 737 (Classic through 800), Boeing 777, Airbus A320 families  
**Compliance Requirement:** Inspect wiring for aging (corrosion, embrittlement) every 2 years or 500 flight hours  
**Conform-Edge Risk Score:** 🔴 **RED** (immediate Part 145 repair station action required)

**What this means for repair stations:**
- If your Part 145 approval includes "Electrical Systems," this AD affects your capabilities
- Boeing 737 and A320 components on your approved list will trigger compliance requirements
- Customers will begin requesting compliance verification (AD compliance reports)

**Your action plan:**
1. Check your Part 145 schedule: Are you approved for Boeing 737 / A320 electrical system work?
2. If YES: Download AD 2026-12-01 from FAA.gov immediately
3. Notify all mechanics on electrical system work (training requirement)
4. Update your work instructions: Include aging-aircraft electrical inspection procedures
5. Document training completion in your training records (FAA auditors will check this)

**Deadline:** Sep 15, 2026 (56 days from now)

**Resources:**
- FAA AD 2026-12-01 (full text): [www.faa.gov/ads/]
- FAA AC 2100-13: AD Applicability Guide

**Estimated compliance labor:** 20–40 hours (procedures + training)

### 1.2 FAA Notice: AC 145-10 Revision Update (Training & Qualifications)
**Type:** Advisory Circular  
**Date Published:** June 28, 2026  
**Topic:** Maintenance Personnel Training & Continuing Qualification (AC 145-10)  
**Key Changes:**
- NEW: "Critical Systems Training" requirement for all Part 145 mechanics (effective Jan 2027)
- NEW: Online training now approved for FAA Part 145 (was previously classroom-only)
- CHANGED: Continuing qualification frequency increased from 24 to 36 months (favorable to MROs)

**Conform-Edge Risk Score:** 🟡 **YELLOW** (training planning required by Dec 2026)

**What this means for repair stations:**
- Your training program needs revision to include "critical systems" module (brakes, flight controls, landing gear = critical)
- You can now deliver training online (reduces costs, improves scheduling)
- Your next surveillance audit will check for AC 145-10 compliance

**Your action plan:**
1. Download AC 145-10 latest revision from FAA.gov
2. Compare your current training program to new critical systems list
3. Identify gaps (what critical systems do you NOT currently train?)
4. If gaps exist: Either (a) update your training to add them, or (b) limit your Part 145 scope to exclude those systems
5. Plan implementation by Dec 31, 2026 (gives you 6 months before Jan 1, 2027 deadline)

**Deadline:** Jan 1, 2027 (full compliance)

**Resources:**
- FAA AC 145-10 (latest): [www.faa.gov/]
- FAA Part 145.163 (Personnel): [14 CFR 145.163]

### 1.3 FAA Enforcement: Part 145 Repeat Violations — 2026 Q2 Summary
**Type:** Enforcement Trend  
**Data Source:** FAA Civil Penalties Database (2026-01 through 2026-06)  
**Summary:** FAA issued 47 CAR notices to Part 145 repair stations in Q1-Q2 2026

**Top 5 violation categories:**
1. **Inadequate training records** (24% of CARs) — Most common FAA finding
2. **Technical data deficiencies** (18%) — OEM manuals not current or complete
3. **Scheduled maintenance non-compliance** (15%) — Aircraft maintenance intervals not followed
4. **Counterfeit/unverifiable parts** (12%) — Supply chain traceability gaps
5. **Maintenance reserve audit** (11%) — FAA Form 337 inconsistencies

**Conform-Edge Risk Analysis:**
- Most violations are procedural (fixable with updated training + documentation)
- Repeat offenders in 2026: Primarily small MROs (<50 employees) with inadequate quality infrastructure

**Your action plan:**
1. Conduct internal audit: Do your training records match FAA § 145.163 requirements?
2. Review your technical data library: Are all manuals current? Are revision dates documented?
3. Check your calibration/maintenance schedule: Is it being followed 100%?
4. Supply chain review: Can you trace ALL parts back to OEM or documented repair history?

**Deadline:** Plan compliance audit by Oct 1, 2026 (ahead of potential FAA surveillance)

## 2. EASA UPDATES (Part-145 Repair Centers)

### 2.1 NEW: EASA Form 8 Enhanced Requirements (MAG Change 10, Section 20)
**Type:** Maintenance Annex Guidance (MAG CHG 10)  
**Effective Date:** October 10, 2025 (already in effect; compliance deadline passed)  
**Topic:** Safety Management System (SMS) / Safety Management & Verification Plan (SMSVP)

**Conform-Edge Risk Score:** 🔴 **RED** (if you haven't updated, you are currently non-compliant)

**What this means:**
- EASA Form 8 audits now require SMS/SMSVP documentation
- FAA Technical Agent will be checking for SMS integration during bilateral audits
- Your EASA-approved repair center must demonstrate SMS is integrated with your QMS

**Your action plan (if not yet done):**
1. Review your current EASA Supplement against MAG CHG 10, Section 20
2. If your SMS/SMSVP is not documented: Create it immediately (your EASA approval is at risk)
3. Integrate SMS into your Quality Manual (show how SMS + QMS work together)
4. Notify your FAA Principal Operations Inspector (if bilaterally approved)

**Deadline:** IMMEDIATE (compliance was due Oct 10, 2025; if you're not in compliance, contact your EASA Competent Authority)

**Resources:**
- EASA Maintenance Annex Guidance (MAG), Change 10: [www.easa.europa.eu/]
- EASA Part-145.A.5 (Quality Management): [EASA regulations]

## 3. CAAC UPDATES (CCAR-145 Repair Centers)

**Type:** Certification Specification  
**Status:** In effect since Jan 1, 2026  
**Topic:** Safety Management System integration with QMS  
**Conform-Edge Risk Score:** 🔴 **RED** (compliance may be incomplete for many Western MROs)

**What this means:**
- CAAC now requires SMS to be integrated into QMS (similar to EASA MAG CHG 10)
- If your CCAR-145 approval predates Jan 1, 2026, your SMS/QMS integration may not be current
- CAAC will check SMS/QMS integration during surveillance audits

**Your action plan:**
1. Review your CCAR-145 compliance manual against CCAR-145 R4 requirements
2. Check: Is SMS clearly integrated with your QMS? Or are they separate?
3. If separate: Merge SMS and QMS documentation (show how they complement each other)
4. If your MRO is in China or Mexicali: Coordinate with your CAAC Competent Authority

**Deadline:** IMMEDIATE (R4 compliance is in effect)

**Resources:**
- CAAC CCAR-145: [www.caac.gov.cn/] (Chinese and English versions available)
- CAAC Technical Advisory Circulars: [CAAC website]

### 3.2 CAAC Requirement: Recovered Component Tagging (R4 Clarification)
**Type:** Regulatory Clarification  
**Date Issued:** June 2026  
**Topic:** Traceability of recovered/refurbished components

**Key Requirement:** All recovered (salvaged, refurbished) components must have:
- Documented proof of airworthiness recovery process
- Traceability to original aircraft (air frame serial number, component part number, hours, cycles)
- CAAC approval tag (yellow tag with CAAC marking)

**Conform-Edge Risk Score:** 🔴 **RED** (affects all distributors and repair stations handling recovered parts)

**What this means:**
- If your MRO salvages/recovers components: You must tag them with CAAC-approved recovery tags
- Customers buying from your MRO will expect CAAC-tagged recovered parts
- Without proper tagging, recovered parts cannot be sold in Chinese market (CAAC will not permit installation)

**Your action plan:**
1. Check: Do you currently salvage/recover components?
2. If YES: Obtain CAAC-approved recovery tags (contact CAAC Competent Authority)
3. Create a procedure: Document the recovery process and tagging requirement
4. Train mechanics: All staff handling recovered components must understand tagging requirement
5. Implement traceability: Log original aircraft serial number, component history

**Deadline:** IMMEDIATE (this is an active CAAC requirement)

**Resources:**
- CAAC CCAR-145.A.36 (Maintenance of Components): Contact your CAAC Competent Authority for forms/tags

## 4. UK CAA UPDATES (CAR-145 Repair Facilities)

### 4.1 UK CAA Publication: CAP 552 Revision 4 (Updated Post-Brexit)
**Type:** CAA Publication  
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026  
**Topic:** Maintenance and Continued Airworthiness  
**Key Changes:**
- NEW: UK-only approval process (no longer automatic EASA approval)
- CHANGED: Continuing Airworthiness Management Organization (CAMO) coordination requirements
- NEW: UK-specific training and qualification requirements

**Conform-Edge Risk Score:** 🟡 **YELLOW** (affects UK repair centers and EU centers with UK approvals)

**What this means:**
- If you have a UK CAR-145 approval: You must ensure compliance with CAP 552 Rev 4
- If you're EU-based and want UK approval: New application process (no longer reciprocal with EASA)
- CAMO coordination requirements are more rigorous than EASA

**Your action plan:**
1. If UK-based: Download CAP 552 Revision 4; compare against your current procedures
2. If EU-based seeking UK approval: Contact UK CAA for new approval application process
3. Review CAMO coordination procedures: Ensure they meet UK CAA expectations

**Deadline:** Jan 1, 2026 (already effective; compliance should be in place)

**Resources:**
- UK CAA CAP 552 (Revision 4): [www.caa.co.uk/]
- UK CAA CAR-145 GMPs: [UK CAA website]

## 5. CASA UPDATES (Part 145 Repair Facilities)

### 5.1 CASA Notice: Part 145 Auditor Qualification Changes (Effective Sept 1, 2026)
**Type:** Regulatory Guidance  
**Date Issued:** June 2026  
**Topic:** Inspector/Auditor qualification requirements for Part 145 MROs

**Key Changes:**
- NEW: Part 145 auditors must have minimum 5 years MRO experience (was 3 years)
- NEW: Auditors must be certified by CASA (new certification program launched June 2026)
- CHANGED: Auditor training curriculum updated (emphasis on SMS/SMS-related risks)

**Conform-Edge Risk Score:** 🟡 **YELLOW** (affects internal audit team composition)

**What this means:**
- If you conduct internal audits: Ensure auditors meet new qualification requirements
- Your internal audit team may need retraining or replacement
- CASA surveillance audits will verify auditor qualifications

**Your action plan:**
1. Check: What are your current auditors' qualifications?
2. Do they have ≥5 years MRO experience + CASA certification?
3. If not: Enroll in new CASA auditor certification program (launched June 2026)
4. Plan transition: Ensure auditors are certified before your next CASA audit

**Deadline:** Sep 1, 2026 (certification must be in place)

**Resources:**
- CASA Part 145: [www.casa.gov.au/]
- CASA Auditor Certification Program: [CASA website; new program details]
