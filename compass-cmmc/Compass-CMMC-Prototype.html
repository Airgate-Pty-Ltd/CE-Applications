The Challenge

Why CMMC is uniquely hard to navigate

CMMC is not a checkbox exercise. It's 110 practices across 14 domains, each with implementation evidence, documentation, and an assessment by a credentialed third party — for a population that largely runs on spreadsheets and a single IT generalist.

💸

The cost is disqualifying

First-year Level 2 compliance costs range from $75K to $300K per organization. Third-party assessment alone runs $50K–$200K, with C3PAO backlogs now 24–30 months deep. Small manufacturers simply cannot absorb this without understanding the problem first.

🧩

The language is opaque

NIST 800-171 was written for federal IT administrators, not shop floor managers. "Establish and maintain baseline configurations" means nothing to an owner who runs MRO work with three engineers. No tool currently translates this in context, at the point of assessment.

📋

Gap reports don't close gaps

Every tool on the market produces a gap analysis. What it does not produce is a path from where you are to where you need to be, specific to your company size, contract types, and existing controls. The report lands and sits.

⏱

Training is disconnected from doing

CMMC training programs and CMMC assessment tools are entirely separate products. You learn in one place, then figure out what to do in another. The conceptual bridge — "here's what this practice means in your context right now" — doesn't exist.

The Framework

Five systems, one guided experience

Compass is not a checklist with a score at the end. It is a structured, progressive workflow that closes the gap between regulatory intent and operational reality — at every step.

01

Onboard

Company profile, contract types, CUI scope, and existing controls. Compass uses this to calibrate guidance throughout — what N/A actually means for you.

02

Assess + Teach

Practice by practice, in plain English. Each practice shows what it means, what evidence looks like, what "partial" vs. "full" looks like for your company size. Answer as you learn.

03

Remediate

Every gap generates a prioritized, specific action list. Not "implement multi-factor authentication" — "here are three MFA options that work with your existing Microsoft 365 environment."

04

Document

SSP, POA&M, and SPRS score generated continuously as you work. Every answer is timestamped, every revision versioned. Your evidence package assembles itself.

05

Audit-Ready

C3PAO interview prep, evidence index, domain-by-domain readiness summary. Compass tells you when you're ready, not when you think you are.

Why this matters

C3PAO assessors consistently cite CM as one of the three most failed domains in DIB assessments. Baseline configurations are foundational — without them, you cannot detect unauthorized changes, which is the entire point of CMMC's configuration management requirements.

✅
What "fully implemented" looks like
  • Complete hardware and software asset inventory, verified quarterly
  • Documented standard configurations for each system type (server, workstation, network device)
  • Process to approve and record deviations from baseline
  • Evidence of last comparison against baseline (change control log)
⚠️
Common assessment failure points
  • Inventory exists but is months out of date
  • Baseline documented for servers but not workstations
  • No process documented for managing exceptions

Why this is still good in five years

The market risk most often cited is regulatory change. That risk is real but bounded: NIST 800-171 Rev 3 is the stable foundation and not subject to political risk in the way a single administration's rule might be. The bigger risk is commoditization — GRC platforms will eventually catch up on CMMC specificity. The moat is not the checklist. It is the teaching layer, the remediation specificity, the evidence management, and the audit-ready output. Those are compounding advantages as Compass accumulates user data on what gaps look like, what remediations work, and what C3PAOs actually examine. A platform that learns what "implemented" looks like across 6,000 defense manufacturers cannot be replicated by bolting a CMMC framework onto a SOC 2 tool.
