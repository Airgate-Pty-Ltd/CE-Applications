ITAR / EAR Export Control Compliance Self-Assessment

ITAR (22 CFR Parts 120–130) · EAR (15 CFR Parts 730–774) · For US Aerospace & Defense Suppliers

Assessment Setup

⚠ This tool identifies compliance gaps for internal use. It is not legal advice and does not constitute an export compliance determination. Consult qualified export counsel for licensing decisions and jurisdictional analysis.
Section 1 — Jurisdiction Determination & Product Classification
1
A formal process exists to determine whether products, technology, or software are subject to ITAR (USML) or EAR (CCL/EAR99) before export, re-export, or transfer (in-country). ⚑ Critical
22 CFR §120.4 (ITAR commodity jurisdiction); 15 CFR §734 (EAR scope)
2
A current product/technology inventory exists with documented USML category (for ITAR items) or ECCN / EAR99 classification (for EAR items) for each item or technology grouping.
22 CFR §121 USML; 15 CFR Supp. 1 to Part 774 CCL
3
When jurisdiction is uncertain, a formal commodity jurisdiction (CJ) request process (ITAR) or a self-classification review against the CCL (EAR) is followed, and results are documented.
22 CFR §120.4; 15 CFR §748.3
4
Technical data controlled under ITAR or EAR is identified and marked (e.g., "ITAR Controlled — Not for Foreign Nationals" or ECCN marking), and access controls are applied.
22 CFR §120.10 (technical data); 15 CFR §734.9
Section 2 — Registration & Export Authorization
5
If the organization manufactures, exports, or brokers ITAR-controlled defense articles, it is registered with the Directorate of Defense Trade Controls (DDTC) and registration is current. ⚑ Critical (ITAR)
22 CFR Part 122 — DDTC Registration
6
Export licenses, agreements (Manufacturing License Agreement, Technical Assistance Agreement), or applicable license exceptions are obtained and on file BEFORE exporting controlled items, technology, or software. ⚑ Critical
22 CFR Part 123 (ITAR licenses); 15 CFR Part 740 (EAR license exceptions)
7
License conditions, reporting obligations, and expiry dates are tracked in a register and managed proactively (alerts 90 days before expiry).
22 CFR §123.26 (license recordkeeping); 15 CFR §762 (EAR records)
8
Shipment records (export documentation, shippers export declarations, EEI filings via AES) are prepared accurately and retained for the required period.
15 CFR §762.2 (5-year retention); Census Bureau AES requirements
Section 3 — Export Management Program (Technology Control Plan / EMCP)
9
A documented Export Management and Compliance Program (EMCP) or Technology Control Plan (TCP) is in place, assigns responsibility, defines controls for controlled technology access, and is reviewed annually. ⚑ Critical
DDTC Best Practices for EMCP; 22 CFR §127.12 (voluntary disclosures)
10
A Restricted Party Screening (RPS) process is in place to check all customers, suppliers, and business partners against applicable denied/debarred/sanctioned party lists (BIS Entity List, DDTC Debarred List, OFAC SDN) before each transaction.
15 CFR Part 744; 22 CFR Part 127; OFAC 31 CFR Part 500+
11
End-use and end-user verification is performed for transactions involving controlled items — end-user certificates (EUC) or destination control statements obtained as required.
15 CFR §758.6 (destination control statement); 22 CFR §123.9 (end-use certificate)
12
Physical access to ITAR/EAR-controlled hardware, technical data, and facilities is controlled — visitors, foreign nationals, and contractors are managed to prevent unauthorized access to controlled technology.
22 CFR §120.10; 22 CFR §120.15 (US person); TCP physical controls
Section 4 — Deemed Exports, Personnel, & Training
13
A deemed export assessment is performed for foreign national employees and contractors with access to controlled technology — EAR-controlled technology released to a foreign national in the US is a deemed export requiring an EAR license if the item would require one to their home country. ⚑ Critical
15 CFR §734.13 (deemed export); 22 CFR §120.17 (ITAR deemed export)
14
All employees with export-related responsibilities (shipping, engineering, sales, IT with CUI access) receive documented export compliance training at hire and on a recurring basis (at minimum annually).
DDTC EMCP Best Practices; BIS enforcement factor — training
15
A designated Export Compliance Officer (or responsible individual) with documented authority and contact information is identified for ITAR/EAR compliance decisions.
DDTC Best Practices; EMCP accountability element
Section 5 — Records, Violation Response & Voluntary Disclosure
16
Export records (licenses, shipping docs, end-user certs, screening results, training records) are retained for a minimum of 5 years in a retrievable format.
22 CFR §122.5 (ITAR — 5 yrs); 15 CFR §762.2 (EAR — 5 yrs)
17
A documented process exists to identify, investigate, and report potential export violations — including voluntary self-disclosure procedures for ITAR (DDTC) and EAR (BIS) to mitigate penalties.
22 CFR §127.12 (ITAR voluntary disclosure); 15 CFR §764.5 (EAR voluntary self-disclosure)
18
The export compliance program includes an internal audit or compliance review conducted at planned intervals to verify effectiveness and identify gaps before an enforcement action.
DDTC EMCP Best Practices; BIS voluntary self-assessment
19
Supply chain / subcontractor flow-down requirements are in place: controlled technology shared with domestic subcontractors includes provisions preventing unauthorized re-export or foreign national access.
22 CFR §123.16; 15 CFR §740 License Exception STA flow-down conditions
