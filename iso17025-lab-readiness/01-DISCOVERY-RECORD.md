## Gateway Questions

**1. Who is the exact buyer and what triggers the purchase?**
Quality manager or lab manager at an aerospace, medical device, or defense supplier operating an in-house calibration laboratory. Trigger: preparing for first ILAC/A2LA/UKAS/NATA accreditation, responding to a customer audit finding that the lab is not accredited, or conducting internal readiness review before a surveillance visit. Distinguishable from the portfolio's Calibration OOT Impact Manager (which supports equipment users; this supports lab operators seeking accreditation).

**2. What outcome does the buyer walk away with?**
A scored readiness profile across all five ISO 17025:2017 major sections (General Requirements, Structural, Resource, Process, Management System); identification of evidence gaps; a prioritized remediation action list; a section-by-section narrative for management review or pre-assessment meeting.

**3. Why does this earn $250k+ per year?**
~5,000–8,000 aerospace and medical device suppliers in North America operate in-house calibration labs; many are under customer or regulatory pressure to achieve accreditation. At $199–$349 one-time + $99/yr maintenance, 800 units/yr = $250k path. Bundle potential with Calibration OOT Impact Manager as a Calibration Compliance Pack.

**4. Is this buildable as a self-contained HTML tool?**
Yes. ISO 17025:2017 has a well-defined 5-section structure with deterministic requirements. No standard text reproduced; requirements referenced by clause number and intent. ~30 questions, 5 sections, 2–3 critical items per section.

**5. Does this conflict with anything in the portfolio?**
No. Calibration OOT Impact Manager (08-14) addresses what to do when a calibrated instrument goes out of tolerance — it is for equipment users. This tool addresses whether the calibration lab itself meets ISO 17025:2017 accreditation requirements — a different scope and buyer.
