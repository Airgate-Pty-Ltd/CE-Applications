## 2. REQUIREMENT-LEVEL TRACEABILITY

Every auditable obligation must retain its exact source reference.

For AS9100D, AS9110, ISO standards, FAA regulations, EASA regulations, and other frameworks, preserve the full applicable hierarchy.

Examples:

- AS9100D §7.2(c)
- AS9100D §8.4.2
- AS9100D §8.5.1(c)(1)
- AS9100D §8.5.1.3
- FAA 14 CFR §145.163, with the appropriate paragraph subdivisions where applicable.

These examples demonstrate reference precision, not a declaration that every example contains a standalone auditable requirement.

**Verify the actual clause structure before using any reference in an audit card or NCR.**

Do not invent paragraph designators.

Do not infer lettered subdivisions from a heading.

Do not truncate a citation at the parent clause when the alleged deficiency concerns a specific subordinate requirement.

Where a requirement contains several separately auditable obligations, the intelligence pack must identify and map each relevant obligation.

Maintain:

- Standard or regulation.
- Exact edition/revision.
- Clause or section identifier.
- Applicable subclause.
- Lettered or numbered requirement.
- Requirement intent.
- Independently authored requirement interpretation.
- Source verification status.
- Content pack version.
- Related internal and customer requirements.

Requirement references must be validated against the authoritative source hierarchy.

A missing, unverified, or ambiguous reference must prevent automatic finalization of an NCR.

## 3. REQUIREMENT INTELLIGENCE MUST SUPPORT DEEP AUDITING

The existing Clause Map Framework must evolve beyond broad clause summaries.

Introduce structured auditable obligations within each Requirement Intelligence Record.

Each auditable obligation should support:

**Requirement Identity**

Exact requirement reference and source version.

**Requirement Intent**

What the governing obligation is intended to accomplish.

**Expected Control**

What type of control, activity, or outcome would satisfy the obligation.

**Verification Objective**

What the auditor must establish to evaluate conformity.

**Verification Method**

Specific interview, observation, record-review, sampling, and process-tracing approaches.

**Detailed Verification Steps**

A sequence of substantive audit activities.

**Expected Objective Evidence**

Evidence types that could demonstrate implementation or effectiveness.

**Sampling Guidance**

Selection considerations, risks, population, and sample rationale.

**Common Control Weaknesses**

Potential failure conditions that deserve investigation but are not automatic findings.

**Related Obligations**

Other relevant standards, regulatory provisions, internal QMS requirements, and customer obligations.

**Evidence and Assessment Links**

Relationships between the obligation, actual evidence, auditor observations, and final conclusion.

**Validation Status**

Source verification, technical review, authoring approval, and content version.

A broad checklist question must never imply that all subordinate obligations have been evaluated when they have not.

## 4. DEFENSIBLE AUDIT CARD DESIGN

The prototype must demonstrate a comprehensive audit card that an experienced aerospace auditor would recognize as a serious audit instrument.

Each card shall contain:

### A. Governing Audit Criterion

Identify the exact requirement down to the relevant lettered or numbered subdivision.

Show source, revision, reference, and applicable internal QMS criteria.

### B. Requirement Intent

Provide a clear independently authored explanation of the intended requirement outcome.

Do not reproduce restricted copyrighted standard text without authorization.

### C. Audit Objective

Explain what conformity or effective implementation must be demonstrated.

### D. Detailed Verification Activities

Provide multiple verification steps tailored to the requirement.

For example, when auditing competence-action effectiveness:

1. Determine how the organization identifies required competence.
2. Identify situations where competence gaps required action.
3. Select completed actions for review.
4. Identify planned effectiveness-evaluation methods.
5. Verify that the evaluations occurred.
6. Compare actual results against defined acceptance criteria.
7. Examine responses to ineffective actions.
8. Verify appropriate records and authorization.
9. Follow up through interviews or work observations where necessary.

These are examples of audit activities, not additional obligations imposed by the standard.

### E. Evidence Expectations

Identify potentially relevant objective evidence and distinguish mandatory documented information from optional or organization-specific implementation records.

Do not incorrectly imply that a particular form, matrix, or template is required when alternative methods can satisfy the requirement.

### F. Sampling

Allow the auditor to record:

- Sample population.
- Population size, if known.
- Sample size.
- Selection method.
- Selection rationale.
- Risk considerations.
- Sample identifiers.
- Records reviewed.
- Samples rejected or replaced.
- Scope and limitations of the sample.

### G. Actual Objective Evidence

Allow precise capture of:

- Document title.
- Document number.
- Revision.
- Record identifier.
- Transaction or work-order number.
- Date.
- Individual role or qualification.
- Equipment identifier.
- Product identifier.
- Supplier or customer identifier.
- Observed condition.
- Interview information.
- Electronic record reference.
- Attached photographs.
- Relevant timestamps.

Evidence must be linked to the specific obligation it supports or contradicts.

### H. Evaluation

Distinguish:

- Requirement demonstrated.
- Requirement potentially not demonstrated.
- Insufficient objective evidence.
- Contradictory evidence requiring resolution.
- Not yet evaluated.
- Justified non-applicability.

The auditor must evaluate the evidence, not merely mark Yes or No.

### I. Related Requirement Assessment

Identify relevant connected requirements without automatically adding findings.

### J. Auditor Conclusion

Retain the auditor's actual conclusion, rationale, evidence references, identity, and timestamp.

The application shall not mark a requirement conforming simply because an attachment exists.

**The presence of a document is not evidence that the underlying process is effective.**

## 5. MANDATORY NCR FORMAT — SHOULD BE / AS FOUND

This is a non-negotiable Conform-Edge auditing requirement.

**Every formal NCR must be constructed using a Should Be / As Found structure.**

### 5.1 SHOULD BE — REQUIREMENT CONDITION

The Should Be section identifies the governing obligation the organization was required to satisfy.

It must contain:

- Governing standard, regulation, contract, or internal QMS source.
- Exact edition or revision.
- Exact clause and subordinate paragraph identifier.
- Clear statement of the applicable requirement.
- Requirement applicability to the audited process.

The language may be accurately paraphrased, provided that:

1. The intended requirement is preserved.
2. No mandatory condition is removed.
3. No additional requirement is invented.
4. The interpretation accurately reflects the applicable obligation.
5. The exact clause reference is preserved.
6. The source and revision are verifiable.

When multiple governing criteria are involved, identify each distinct requirement rather than combining unrelated obligations into a vague citation.

The Should Be must not be constructed merely from what the auditor thinks constitutes good practice.

### 5.2 AS FOUND — OBJECTIVELY VERIFIED CONDITION

The As Found section describes the actual condition identified during the audit.

It must contain:

- What was observed.
- Where the condition was observed.
- Relevant record or sample identifiers.
- Dates and other necessary traceability.
- Actual results or conditions.
- Specific evidence supporting the observation.
- The verified difference between the observed condition and the governing requirement.

Avoid vague statements such as:

- Training is inadequate.
- Supplier control is poor.
- Procedure not followed.
- Management failed to ensure compliance.
- Records were insufficient.

These statements are not independently adequate objective evidence.

The As Found must establish a factual, supportable condition.

### 5.3 REQUIRED NCR CONSTRUCTION

Every NCR must include the following structured elements:

**NCR Number:** Controlled unique identifier.

**Audit Reference:** Audit ID, organization, process, and site.

**Governing Requirement:** Exact source and clause reference.

**Should Be:** Accurate paraphrase or permitted quotation of the governing requirement.

**As Found:** Objective condition observed, including precise sample or record identifiers.

**Objective Evidence:** Evidence identifiers supporting the As Found.

**Statement of Nonconformity:** Concise explanation of the gap between the required and observed conditions.

**Classification:** Major, Minor, or other category determined by the applicable audit scheme.

**Auditor:** Name/identifier and date.

**Review/Approval:** Authorized disposition and approval record.

**Status:** Draft, Pending Evidence, Under Review, Approved, Issued, or other controlled states.

Additional fields may support containment, correction, corrective action, verification, and closure in later workflows.

## 6. NCR EXAMPLE — COMPETENCE EFFECTIVENESS

Use the following as an illustrative training scenario, not as actual audit evidence.

### Governing Requirement

AS9100D, Revision D, Clause 7.2(c).

### SHOULD BE

Where actions are taken to obtain necessary personnel competence, the organization is required, as applicable, to evaluate the effectiveness of those actions.

### AS FOUND

During the review of five completed competence-development actions associated with production and inspection personnel, two selected records did not contain evidence that the effectiveness of the completed actions had been evaluated.

The affected records were:

- TR-2026-014, completed September 14, 2026.
- TR-2026-019, completed September 21, 2026.

For both records, training completion was documented; however, no effectiveness-evaluation result was identified in the reviewed records or the designated qualification system.

The responsible supervisor also confirmed during the audit that effectiveness evaluations had not been completed for these two actions.

### STATEMENT OF NONCONFORMITY

The organization did not demonstrate evaluation of the effectiveness of competence-development actions for two of the five sampled completed actions, contrary to the applicable requirement of AS9100D §7.2(c).

### IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION

This example assumes the auditor has verified that:

- The actions were taken to acquire necessary competence.
- An effectiveness evaluation was applicable.
- The records had reached the stage where an evaluation should have been completed.
- The organization had been given an appropriate opportunity to provide alternative objective evidence.
- The supporting evidence establishes an actual unmet obligation rather than merely a missing preferred form.

The example is synthetic.

Do not treat lack of one particular document as automatic proof that a required activity was not performed.

The final finding must be based on the evidence actually established.

## 7. FORMAL NCR GATEKEEPING

An NCR must not be available for final issuance until the following conditions are satisfied:

1. The governing source is identified.
2. The exact clause hierarchy is verified.
3. The requirement is applicable.
4. The Should Be accurately represents the obligation.
5. The As Found identifies an actual condition.
6. Relevant objective evidence is linked.
7. The identified difference constitutes an unmet requirement.
8. Material contradictory or exculpatory evidence has been evaluated.
9. The classification follows the applicable audit scheme.
10. An authorized auditor reviews and approves the finding.

The application should identify incomplete or unsupported findings.

Example:

**Finding Review Required**

"Exact governing paragraph is not verified. Confirm the requirement reference before issuing this NCR."

Or:

**Insufficient Evidence**

"The record is unavailable, but the audit has not established whether the required activity was performed or whether alternative evidence exists."

The application should support good professional audit judgment instead of encouraging premature findings.

## 8. MAK AND M-AI BACKGROUND RESPONSIBILITIES

MAK and M-AI remain invisible to the customer.

### MAK Responsibilities

MAK should assist internally with:

- Selecting relevant verification steps.
- Identifying distinct auditable obligations.
- Recommending representative audit samples.
- Evaluating evidence sufficiency.
- Identifying contradictory evidence.
- Recognizing potential unmet requirements.
- Proposing appropriate follow-up questions.
- Identifying related requirement relationships.
- Preparing draft Should Be / As Found statements.
- Reviewing whether the draft NCR is logically supported.

### M-AI Responsibilities

M-AI should govern:

- Whether source references are verified.
- Whether an obligation is applicable.
- Whether evidence access is authorized.
- Whether an NCR meets its issuance prerequisites.
- Whether an inference is improperly presented as fact.
- Whether a recommendation exceeds approved scope.
- Whether an auditor approval is required.
- Whether historical records are protected.
- Whether licensed content may be displayed or exported.

Neither system may silently issue an NCR or alter an auditor-approved conclusion.

The customer-facing application should display ordinary professional language such as:

"Finding requires additional supporting evidence."

"Requirement reference verified."

"Potential related requirement identified."

"Ready for auditor review."

Do not expose MAK or M-AI names, internal policy codes, or proprietary architecture.

## 10. USER EXPERIENCE REQUIREMENTS

Depth must not come at the expense of usability.

The audit screen should use progressive disclosure.

Show the auditor:

- Current process.
- Exact requirement reference.
- Current verification activity.
- Evidence status.
- Sample progress.
- Findings requiring attention.
- Next appropriate action.

Detailed explanations, related criteria, and technical reasoning should be expandable.

The auditor must be able to move quickly between:

**Requirement → Verification → Sample → Evidence → Assessment → Finding**

Avoid forcing the auditor through multiple unrelated screens simply to record one observation.

The application should support efficient field auditing while retaining sufficient depth for technical defensibility.

## 11. REQUIRED PROTOTYPE ACCEPTANCE SCENARIOS

Before handoff to Code, demonstrate the following scenarios.

**Scenario A — Conforming**

Evidence demonstrates the applicable requirement was fulfilled.

The auditor records the basis for conformity.

**Scenario B — Potential Nonconformity**

Evidence suggests a control failure, but an important fact remains unverified.

The application retains the issue as pending and recommends the necessary investigation.

**Scenario C — Substantiated Nonconformity**

The exact obligation is verified, objective evidence establishes an unmet requirement, and the application generates a defensible Should Be / As Found NCR for authorized auditor review.

**Scenario D — Incorrect Citation**

A draft NCR references a parent clause when the actual obligation is contained in a subordinate paragraph.

The application identifies the insufficient citation and prevents issuance.

**Scenario E — Alternative Compliant Method**

The organization meets the intended requirement using a valid alternative method rather than the form or process the auditor initially expected.

The application recognizes that a preferred implementation method is not necessarily mandatory and supports a conforming assessment.

**Scenario F — Insufficient Evidence**

The auditor has not obtained enough information to establish conformity or nonconformity.

The application prevents an unsupported conclusion and identifies what remains unresolved.

These scenarios must operate through the actual interactive prototype, not merely appear as static examples.
