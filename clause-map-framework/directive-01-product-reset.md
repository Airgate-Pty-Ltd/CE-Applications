# 5. REQUIRED INTELLIGENCE ARCHITECTURE

Integrate MAK and M-AI as core architectural capabilities through controlled, versioned contracts.

## 5.1 MAK — Audit Intelligence

MAK should provide the application's advanced domain reasoning.

Its target responsibilities include:

**Intent Based Auditing**

Evaluate whether an organization's actual processes, controls, and demonstrated outcomes satisfy the intent of the applicable requirement.

Do not confuse lack of textual similarity with lack of conformity.

An organization can be compliant even when its documentation or terminology differs from the standard.

**Adaptive Questioning**

Generate contextually appropriate follow-up questions based on:

- Audit objectives.
- Applicable requirements.
- Evidence reviewed.
- Identified risks.
- Organization type.
- Process complexity.
- Prior findings.
- Current observations.
- Unresolved uncertainties.

**Evidence Triangulation**

Compare multiple evidence sources to identify agreement, gaps, discrepancies, and contradictions.

Distinguish confirmed facts from interpretations.

**Cross-Framework Intelligence**

Identify relevant relationships among standards, regulations, customer requirements, internal procedures, and operational controls.

Maintain the authority and applicability of each individual source.

**Historical Audit Intelligence**

Use prior audits, findings, corrective actions, recurring trends, and approved organizational context to recommend more effective investigations.

Prior findings are leads for investigation, not automatic proof of present nonconformity.

**Risk-Based Sampling**

Recommend audit samples based on process risk, criticality, previous performance, product safety, changes, and audit objectives.

Sampling recommendations must distinguish professional judgment from mandatory requirements.

**Investigation Intelligence**

Support evaluation of potential systemic weaknesses, recurring failures, ineffective corrective actions, and conflicting evidence.

**Audit Reporting Intelligence**

Help assemble clear, technically defensible, evidence-linked findings and executive reporting.

**Governed Skills**

MAK may use approved specialized auditor skills, subject to versioning, compatibility tests, validation, and permission controls.

Do not assume these capabilities are already implemented.

Inspect the actual MAK implementation and establish a verified baseline.

## 5.2 M-AI — Decision Governance

M-AI provides the application's bounded decision architecture.

Its internal decision outcomes are:

- PROCEED
- HOLD
- BLOCK
- ADVISE

These outcomes control whether a proposed system action may proceed, requires additional evidence, must be blocked, or should remain advisory.

They are NOT compliance classifications.

M-AI should govern:

- Source trust.
- Data integrity.
- Application permissions.
- Content licensing.
- Evidence provenance.
- Decision confidence.
- Recommendation eligibility.
- Audit scope enforcement.
- Human approval requirements.
- Protection of immutable audit records.
- Controlled use of external intelligence services.
- Policy and version compatibility.

M-AI must never manufacture evidence, silently change an authoritative requirement, override a deterministic applicability rule without authorized review, or convert an inference into a verified fact.

## 5.3 Architectural Separation

Implement three layers:

**Device Layer**

Contains the user experience, audit execution, local data, approved requirement packs, deterministic applicability, essential policy enforcement, evidence capture, and offline functionality.

**Audit Intelligence Services**

Contains approved MAK-related reasoning capabilities and server-side intelligence functions, subject to customer privacy, authentication, and deployment policy.

**Governed Development and Release Environment**

Contains proprietary intelligence development, skill evaluation, policy authoring, testing, improvement, and release approval.

Do not embed the full private MAK development environment into the customer application.

Do not duplicate proprietary source code unnecessarily.

Use explicit versioned interfaces and adapters.

If existing MAK or M-AI repositories do not provide suitable production services, create contracts and local test stubs without misrepresenting their status.

# 6. THE APPLICATION MUST BE AN AUDIT SYSTEM, NOT A CHECKLIST

The primary audit model must be process-based, context-aware, and evidence-driven.

Requirements establish the governing audit criteria.

Processes establish how the audit is performed.

Evidence establishes what was observed.

Auditor judgment establishes the formal conclusion.

The application must support both:

- Traditional clause-based auditing.
- Process-based auditing with multiple applicable requirements.

Process-based auditing should be the preferred experience where appropriate.

For example:

**Receiving Inspection**

The application may connect:

- Supplier approval.
- Purchase-order requirements.
- Receiving verification.
- Material identification.
- Traceability.
- Counterfeit prevention.
- Inspection status.
- Storage.
- Production release.
- Nonconforming material control.

The auditor should move naturally through this process.

The system should display relevant requirements and evidence expectations at the appropriate points.

It should not force the auditor to manually navigate every clause in sequence.

- Do not redistribute unauthorized requirement text.

- Confirm source authenticity.
- Verify edition and effective date.

- Validate applicability.
- Maintain version controls.
