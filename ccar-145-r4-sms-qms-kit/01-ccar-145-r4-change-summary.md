## 1. Context: Why R4 Combines SMS and QMS

CAAC CCAR-145 Revision 4 aligns with ICAO Annex 19 (2nd Edition, 2016) which designated ICAO Member States to require SMS for maintenance organizations above prescribed thresholds. China's R4 implements this obligation by formally embedding the four ICAO SMS components into the CCAR-145 QMS structure rather than establishing a standalone parallel system.

The practical effect: **Your QMS and SMS are now one integrated management framework.** Organizations that previously managed them separately must now reconcile, merge, or clearly cross-reference the two.

---

## 2. The Four ICAO SMS Components (now required inside your CCAR-145 QMS)

### Component 1: Safety Policy and Objectives
**What ICAO requires:** Accountable Manager commits to SMS. Safety policy signed, communicated, visible. Safety objectives set at the organizational level.

**What R4 adds to your QMS:**
- Safety policy must be co-located with (or integrated into) your Quality Policy
- Safety objectives become formal documented commitments alongside quality objectives
- Accountable Manager's safety accountability must be explicitly documented in the AMO Manual
- A **Safety Review Board** (or equivalent named body) is required for AMOs above a threshold size

**Common gap:** Organizations that had a safety policy buried in an ops manual rather than integrated into the management system will face a finding.

### Component 2: Safety Risk Management
**What ICAO requires:** Hazard identification process. Risk assessment methodology. Documented mitigations before beginning operations with known hazards.

**What R4 adds to your QMS:**
- Hazard identification must be integrated into the existing nonconformance / discrepancy reporting system
- A **Hazard Register** is a new required documented record (no equivalent in prior CCAR-145 QMS requirements)
- Risk assessment for safety hazards must follow a defined methodology — the CCAR-145 R4 model uses a 5×5 severity/likelihood matrix **[PLACEHOLDER: confirm matrix dimensions from class materials]**
- Safety risk assessments must be triggered by: accidents/incidents, near-misses, significant process changes, audit findings flagged as safety-critical, and new customer/fleet types

**Common gap:** Organizations using "corrective action" systems for discrepancy reporting without a safety-classification layer will need to add a risk-classification step to their existing process.

### Component 3: Safety Assurance
**What ICAO requires:** Ongoing monitoring of safety performance. Safety data collection and analysis. Internal evaluation (safety audit function). Management of change for safety-impacting changes.

**What R4 adds to your QMS:**
- Safety Performance Indicators (SPIs) must be established, monitored, and trended alongside quality KPIs
- Internal quality audits must include a safety lens — auditors must be trained in SMS concepts
- A **Safety Performance Monitoring Report** is required at defined intervals (minimum: annual; recommended: quarterly)
- Management of Change (MoC) process must specifically flag changes with potential safety impact for hazard assessment before implementation

**Common gap:** Organizations that conduct quality audits without safety criteria, or track maintenance error rates without classifying them by safety consequence, will need to expand their audit scope and KPI dashboard.

### Component 4: Safety Promotion
**What ICAO requires:** Safety training for all personnel (role-differentiated). Safety communication. A positive safety culture with mechanisms for voluntary reporting.

**What R4 adds to your QMS:**
- Training records must include SMS/safety awareness training for all staff, not just technical staff
- A **Just Culture policy** or equivalent voluntary reporting mechanism must be documented
- Safety communication (newsletters, briefings, safety alerts) must be documented as controlled records
- Safety lessons learned from internal incidents/near-misses must be demonstrably shared with affected work groups

**Common gap:** Organizations that provided informal safety briefings without controlled records, or that lack a defined non-punitive reporting channel, will face findings on Component 4.

## 3. New Documentation Requirements Summary

| New Document/Record | Prior Required? | R4 Required? | Notes |
|---------------------|----------------|--------------|-------|
| Safety Policy (integrated) | No | **Yes** | Must be signed, dated, communicated |
| Safety Objectives | No | **Yes** | Measurable, reviewed at management reviews |
| Hazard Register | No | **Yes** | Living record; formal review cycle required |
| Safety Risk Assessment Records | No | **Yes** | Per event, per change, per new operation |
| Safety Performance Indicators | No | **Yes** | Minimum: safety occurrence rate, serious incident count |
| Safety Performance Monitoring Report | No | **Yes** | Tracks SPI trends; reviewed by Accountable Manager |
| SMS Training Records | No | **Yes** | For all personnel, not just technical |
| Just Culture / Voluntary Reporting Procedure | No | **Yes** | Non-punitive; formal channel documented |
| Management of Change (safety) | No | **Yes** | Triggered by qualifying changes |

## 4. What Did NOT Change

- Core certification categories (A1, A2, A3, B1, B2, C, D) — unchanged
- Authorization process for component capabilities — unchanged  
- Certifying Staff qualification requirements — unchanged
- Base technical requirements for maintenance tasks — unchanged
- Customer interface documentation (work orders, maintenance releases) — largely unchanged
