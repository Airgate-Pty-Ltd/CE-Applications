Component 1 — Safety Policy & Objectives
10 requirements · CCAR-145 R4 Section 4 · ICAO Annex 19 §3.1

A1
A signed Safety Policy exists at the organizational level, stating management's commitment to safety and the priority of safety over operational/commercial pressures.
A2
The Safety Policy is personally signed by the Accountable Manager (Post Holder responsible for the entire CAAC-authorized organization) — not delegated to a subordinate signature.
A3
The Safety Policy is communicated to, understood by, and accessible to all personnel (including contract workers and subcontractors); evidence of communication exists (briefing records, acknowledgment forms, posting records).
A4
The Safety Policy is formally reviewed at least annually; the review is documented with date, reviewer(s), and outcome (approved unchanged / updated); version control is maintained.
A5
Documented Safety Objectives exist, are quantified (measurable targets), and are aligned with the Safety Policy; objectives are specific enough to allow evaluation of whether they have been met.
A6
Safety Objectives are reviewed at formal Management Review meetings; progress against objectives is assessed and decisions documented; objectives are updated based on performance data.
A7
The Accountable Manager's safety accountabilities and authority are explicitly stated in the AMO Manual (or integrated SMS+QMS Manual); the Accountable Manager accepts ultimate responsibility for safety outcomes.
A8
The Safety Policy is integrated with or explicitly cross-referenced to the Quality Policy; both are consistent in language, priority, and intent — no conflicting statements exist between the two policies.
A9

A10
Safety roles, responsibilities, and authorities are documented for all key positions in the organization; responsibilities are assigned to named role types (not just individuals) and are reflected in the organizational structure chart in the AMO Manual.
Component 2 — Safety Risk Management (Hazard ID & Risk)
12 requirements · CCAR-145 R4 · ICAO Annex 19 §3.2
0 / 36
—
ICAO 5×5 Risk Matrix (standard for CCAR-145 R4): Severity (Catastrophic / Hazardous / Major / Minor / Negligible) × Likelihood (Frequent / Occasional / Remote / Improbable / Extremely Improbable). Risk acceptability: Intolerable → Tolerable → Acceptable. Verify AMO's matrix is consistent with CAAC/ICAO guidance.
B1
A formal Hazard Identification process exists (documented procedure), distinct from the general discrepancy/nonconformance reporting system; the process defines what constitutes a "hazard" in the maintenance context.
B2
A Hazard Register is maintained as a living, controlled document; it contains all identified hazards, associated risks, current mitigations, risk owners, and residual risk ratings; the register has a defined review frequency.
B3
Hazard identification is systematically triggered by accidents, incidents, and maintenance errors; the process captures causal factors, not just the event itself.
B4
Hazard identification is systematically triggered by near-misses and voluntary safety reports from personnel; these are treated as equal inputs to formal incident investigations.
B5
Hazard identification is triggered by significant process or capability changes (Management of Change); no significant organizational or process change proceeds without a documented hazard identification step.
B6
Hazard identification is triggered by new fleet types, new customers, new approved capabilities, or new work environments; the MoC procedure lists these triggers explicitly.
B7
A documented Risk Assessment methodology is defined using a severity × likelihood matrix; risk level categories (Intolerable / Tolerable / Acceptable) are defined with explicit acceptability thresholds; the matrix is referenced in the AMO Manual.
B8
Risk assessments are completed before implementing changes with identified hazard potential; evidence shows that hazard identification and risk assessment precede — not follow — the change implementation.
B9
Risk mitigation actions are documented in the Hazard Register or linked records, with assigned owners, target dates, and completion status; open mitigations are tracked to closure.
B10
Residual risk is formally assessed and accepted at the appropriate authority level (defined in the SMS procedure); residual risk acceptance is documented with the name and role of the accepting authority.
B11
Safety risk records are retained per a defined retention period specified in the SMS procedure; records are retrievable and protected from unauthorized modification.
B12
The nonconformance/discrepancy reporting system includes a safety-classification layer; all nonconformances are evaluated for safety significance before disposition; safety-classified NCs are escalated per defined criteria.
Component 3 — Safety Assurance
12 requirements · CCAR-145 R4 · ICAO Annex 19 §3.3
0 / 36
—
C1
Safety Performance Indicators (SPIs) are defined, documented, and linked to Safety Objectives; each SPI has a defined alert level (threshold triggering investigation) and a target level (desired performance goal).
C2
SPIs are monitored and trended at defined intervals (minimum: quarterly tracking); trend charts with at least 4 consecutive periods are maintained; the data source for each SPI is identified.
C3
SPI performance is reported to the Accountable Manager on a defined schedule; reports show trend direction, alert threshold status, and recommended actions where alerts are triggered.
C4
A Safety Performance Monitoring Report (or integrated Safety/Quality Management Review report) is produced on a defined schedule (minimum annually); the report includes SPI trends, hazard register review, incident analysis, and action plan status.
C5
The internal audit program includes SMS-specific audit criteria (not just QMS/CCAR-145 technical criteria); auditors evaluate the effectiveness of SMS processes, not just the existence of documents.
C6
Internal auditors assigned to SMS audits have received SMS-specific training; their training records confirm competence in SMS concepts (hazard ID, risk assessment, safety assurance, safety promotion).
C7
A formal Management of Change (MoC) process exists for safety-impacting organizational, process, equipment, and personnel changes; the MoC process is documented in the AMO Manual as a controlled procedure.
C8
MoC records demonstrate that safety hazard assessments were completed and risks were evaluated before changes were implemented; MoC records are retrievable and reference the relevant Hazard Register entries.
C9
Safety data is systematically collected from multiple sources: maintenance errors, maintenance incidents, near-misses, aircraft defect data, voluntary safety reports, and audit findings; data collection is proactive, not solely reactive.
C10
Safety data analysis identifies trends and systemic issues (not just one-off events); trend analysis results are used to update the Hazard Register, adjust SPIs, and inform management decisions.
C11
Audit findings can be classified by safety significance; the classification system distinguishes safety-critical findings (requiring immediate action) from quality findings (managed through standard CAR process).
C12
Safety corrective actions are tracked and managed — either through a dedicated Safety Action tracking system or a classified safety category within the existing CAR/CAPA system; safety actions have shorter response time targets than routine quality CARs.
Component 4 — Safety Promotion
10 requirements · CCAR-145 R4 · ICAO Annex 19 §3.4
0 / 30
—
D1
SMS/safety awareness training has been completed by ALL personnel — not only technical staff; training records confirm coverage of administrative, support, and management personnel as well as maintenance technicians.
D2
SMS training records are maintained for all personnel; records show training topic, date, trainer/provider, and assessment result (where applicable); records are current (recurrent training completed on schedule).
D3
Role-differentiated SMS training exists; training content differs appropriately between managers (accountability, resource allocation, safety culture), technicians (hazard ID, reporting, procedural compliance), and administrative staff (safety reporting channels, non-punitive policy).
D4
A Just Culture policy or non-punitive reporting commitment is documented and distributed to all personnel; the policy explicitly states what behaviors are protected (unintentional errors) and what are not (gross negligence, deliberate violations, substance use).
D5
A voluntary safety reporting channel is operational (paper form, digital system, or equivalent); evidence of actual voluntary reports exists in the system — the existence of voluntary reports indicates the Just Culture is perceived as real by personnel.
D6
Safety communication is documented in a retrievable form (meeting minutes, safety bulletins, email broadcasts, toolbox talks, shift briefings); the communication method is consistent and reaches all shift patterns and work locations.
D7
Lessons learned from incidents and near-misses are demonstrably communicated to affected work groups; communication records show the specific lesson and the specific recipients; feedback loop to reporters is evidenced.
D8
Safety communication records are retained per the defined retention period in the SMS procedure; records are indexed and retrievable; communication records can be linked to specific incidents or hazards that prompted them.
D9
Management visibly champions safety culture — records exist of leadership safety walks, safety briefings led by the Accountable Manager or senior managers, or formal management communications on safety topics; these are not solely reactive to incidents.
D10
New hire onboarding includes SMS and safety culture orientation before the individual begins unsupervised work; training content covers the AMO's Just Culture policy, safety reporting channels, and basic hazard identification for maintenance environments.
