ISO 9001:2026 Transition Readiness Assessor

FDIS Published · Sept 2026 Release

ISO 9001:2026 is expected to publish in September 2026, replacing ISO 9001:2015. Certified organizations have a 3-year transition window (until ~September 2029). The revision integrates climate change into organizational context, adds explicit quality culture and ethical behavior requirements, and addresses digitalization and AI readiness. 1   Organizational Context & Climate Change

Clauses 4.1 – 4.2
1
Has your organization explicitly assessed how climate change affects your ability to consistently provide conforming products or services?
ISO 9001:2026 Clause 4.1 now requires climate change considerations as part of organizational context — a new mandatory input not present in the 2015 version.
2
Are climate-related risks and opportunities documented and integrated into your QMS context analysis?
The climate amendment requires organizations to treat climate risks (supply disruption, resource availability, regulatory change) the same as other external factors driving QMS scope.
3
Do your interested party needs and expectations (Clause 4.2) include sustainability and climate-related requirements?
Customers, regulators, and investors increasingly impose sustainability obligations. ISO 9001:2026 requires these to be identified and monitored as interested party expectations.
4
Is your QMS scope statement reviewed in light of digitalization capabilities and constraints that affect your processes?
ISO 9001:2026 encourages organizations to reflect their digital capability posture within QMS scope. Gaps in digital readiness can materially affect scope boundaries.

2   Leadership, Quality Culture & Ethical Behavior

Clause 5
5
Does top management actively demonstrate commitment to a quality culture — not just sign the quality policy?
ISO 9001:2026 Clause 5.1.1 now explicitly requires top management to promote and demonstrate a quality culture. A signed policy alone no longer satisfies this clause.
6
Are ethical behavior expectations formally communicated, trained, and monitored within your quality management system?
Ethical conduct is a new explicit leadership requirement in the 2026 revision. Organizations must evidence that ethics and integrity are integrated into QMS activities — not just stated in policy.
7
Does your quality policy and management review explicitly address quality culture outcomes (e.g., near-miss reporting culture, speaking-up behaviors)?
Quality culture goes beyond procedures — it's about behaviors. Auditors under 2026 will seek behavioral evidence of culture, not just documented commitments.
8
Can your organization provide objective evidence that quality culture is measured — e.g., culture surveys, quality climate assessments, or behavioral KPIs?
The 2026 revision shifts from "state your commitment" to "show the outcome." Culture measurement is the evidence auditors will look for.

3   Risk Planning & Digitalization Strategy

Clause 6
9
Are climate-related risks (supply chain disruption, resource scarcity, regulatory change) included in your Clause 6.1 risk register?
ISO 9001:2026 requires climate inputs from 4.1 to flow into risk and opportunity management. If climate isn't in your risk register, this is a new mandatory gap.
10
Are digitalization opportunities — including AI, automation, and data analytics — formally assessed as QMS opportunities (Clause 6.1)?
ISO 9001:2026 introduces formal digital readiness requirements. Organizations that have not assessed digitalization as a quality opportunity have a gap in planning.
11
Does your QMS planning address how technology changes (new software, automation tools, AI adoption) affect process risk?
Technology change is now a risk management input. Unplanned technology adoption without QMS consideration is a gap the 2026 standard explicitly addresses.
12
Are supply continuity risks reassessed considering both climate disruption (extreme weather, resource scarcity) and digital disruption (cyberattack, system failure)?
ISO 9001:2026 planning expects organizations to look at compounded risk scenarios — climate and digital — not just traditional supply risk factors.

4   Digital Competence & Documented Information

Clause 7
13
Is digital literacy assessed as part of competence requirements (Clause 7.2) for quality-critical roles?
ISO 9001:2026 requires digital competency to be evaluated — not just technical job skills. Staff using quality-critical digital tools without assessed digital literacy is a gap.
14
Are personnel trained on the relevant digital tools (eQMS, analytics, automation) that affect their quality-critical responsibilities?
Awareness under Clause 7.3 now extends to digital tools and their quality implications. Untrained use of digital systems creates documented information integrity risk.
15
Does your documented information management approach (Clause 7.5) accommodate digital records, including version control, access management, and backup for e-based records?
ISO 9001:2026 Clause 7.5 guidance addresses digital documentation integrity. Paper-era controls applied to digital systems without adequate update are a gap.
16
Is there a documented strategy — even a brief one — for technology adoption as it relates to quality processes?
ISO 9001:2026 encourages organizations to document how they approach technology adoption. Ad-hoc or undocumented adoption creates audit risk under the new revision.

5   Performance Monitoring & Continual Improvement

Clauses 9 – 10
17
Does your analysis of data (Clause 9.1.3) include sustainability or climate-related performance indicators alongside traditional quality metrics?
ISO 9001:2026 extends performance analysis to include sustainability data as a quality indicator — particularly where climate or ESG factors affect product/service conformity.
18
Does your management review input (Clause 9.3) include climate context status, quality culture indicators, and digital performance data?
The 2026 revision's new inputs (climate, culture, digital) must flow into management review. A management review template not updated for these items is non-conforming.
19
Does your internal audit program (Clause 9.2) include assessment of digital readiness, climate risk controls, and quality culture effectiveness?
Internal audits under ISO 9001:2026 must cover the new topic areas. Audit programs not yet updated to include digital and climate dimensions will generate surveillance NCs.
20
Are continual improvement initiatives (Clause 10) documented for climate adaptation and digital transformation — not just traditional corrective action?
ISO 9001:2026 broadens the scope of improvement to explicitly include climate and digital adaptation. Organizations that limit improvement to CAPA miss this new requirement.
