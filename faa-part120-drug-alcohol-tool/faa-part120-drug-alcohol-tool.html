FAA Part 120 Drug & Alcohol Compliance Tool

FAA Part 120 49 CFR Part 40 Self-assessment for FAA-regulated employers under DOT/FAA drug and alcohol testing requirements.

Scope: This tool covers FAA-regulated employers operating under 14 CFR Parts 121, 135, and 145 who must maintain a DOT/FAA-compliant drug and alcohol testing program per 14 CFR Part 120 and 49 CFR Part 40.

Answer YES, NO, or N/A for each item. Items marked CRITICAL will trigger a NON-COMPLIANT verdict if answered NO, regardless of overall score. N/A answers are excluded from scoring.
1
Program Administration
Policy, MRO, DER, Coverage
1. The organization has a written drug and alcohol testing policy that addresses all program elements required by 14 CFR Part 120.
14 CFR §120.105, §120.215 | FAA Order 9790.1
2. The policy has been communicated to all safety-sensitive employees and is available in the workplace.
14 CFR §120.111, §120.221
3. A qualified Medical Review Officer (MRO) has been designated or contracted for the program.
49 CFR §40.121 | 14 CFR §120.107
★ CRITICAL — MRO designation is mandatory; absence = NON-COMPLIANT
4. The MRO holds current DOT credentials (physician licensure, completion of DOT MRO qualification training).
49 CFR §40.121(a)–(c)
5. A Designated Employer Representative (DER) has been identified with authority to act on behalf of the employer in all program matters.
49 CFR §40.3 | 14 CFR §120.105
★ CRITICAL — DER designation is mandatory
6. All safety-sensitive functions covered by 14 CFR §120.105/§120.215 are correctly identified and employees in those roles are enrolled in the program.
14 CFR §120.105 (drug) / §120.215 (alcohol)
2
Testing Requirements
Pre-employment, Post-accident, Reasonable Suspicion, Return-to-Duty
7. Pre-employment drug testing is conducted before any employee performs a safety-sensitive function for the first time.
14 CFR §120.109(a) | 49 CFR §40.25
8. The organization queries the FAA Drug Abatement Division or the previous employer drug/alcohol record for all new safety-sensitive employees prior to first performance.
14 CFR §120.113 | 49 CFR §40.25
9. Post-accident drug AND alcohol testing is conducted per DOT criteria: fatal accidents always; non-fatal per §120.117/§120.227 thresholds.
14 CFR §120.117 / §120.227 | 49 CFR Part 40 Subpart K
★ CRITICAL — Post-accident testing failures are a primary FAA enforcement trigger
10. Reasonable suspicion testing is conducted when a trained supervisor observes specific, contemporaneous, articulable observations of appearance, behavior, speech, or body odors consistent with drug or alcohol use.
14 CFR §120.119 / §120.223 | 49 CFR §40.61
11. Return-to-duty testing is conducted following any violation before the employee returns to safety-sensitive duties, and follow-up testing (minimum 6 tests in 12 months per SAP direction) is in place.
14 CFR §120.111(c) | 49 CFR §§40.305, 40.307
3
Random Testing Program
Pool integrity, rates, selection, and execution
12. Random drug testing is conducted at a minimum DOT rate (currently 25% of safety-sensitive employees annually); random alcohol testing at a minimum 10% rate.
14 CFR §120.109(c) / §120.219 | 49 CFR §40.87
★ CRITICAL — Minimum random testing rates are regulatory floors; rates below minimums = NON-COMPLIANT
13. Selection for random testing uses a truly random method (e.g., computer-generated) ensuring every covered employee has an equal probability of selection in each period.
49 CFR §40.87(b) | FAA AC 120-84
14. Selected employees are tested without advance notice, and testing is conducted immediately before, during, or after performing safety-sensitive functions.
14 CFR §120.109(c)(3) | 49 CFR §40.87(d)
15. Random testing pools are maintained accurately — all safety-sensitive employees included, no non-covered employees included, and additions/deletions are kept current.
49 CFR §40.87 | 14 CFR §120.109
4
Collection, Testing Panel & Laboratory
Collection sites, panels, NLCP lab requirements
16. Drug testing uses DHHS-certified laboratories only (National Laboratory Certification Program); the current lab certification is confirmed on file.
49 CFR §40.81 | 49 CFR Part 40 Subpart F
17. The drug testing panel tests for all 5 DOT drug classes: marijuana, cocaine, opioids, amphetamines/methamphetamines, and phencyclidine (PCP).
49 CFR §40.85 | DHHS Mandatory Guidelines
18. Collection is performed at a qualified collection site using DOT-approved Federal Custody and Control Forms (CCF); chain of custody is maintained from collection to lab.
49 CFR §§40.45–40.73 | 49 CFR Part 40 Subpart D
19. Alcohol testing uses evidential breath testing (EBT) devices on the current NHTSA Conforming Products List, operated by a trained breath alcohol technician (BAT) or screening test technician (STT).
49 CFR §§40.221–40.233 | 49 CFR Part 40 Subpart L
5
Training Requirements
Employee education, supervisor training, SAP referral
20. All safety-sensitive employees have received initial drug and alcohol awareness education covering: effects on performance, the policy, consequences of violation, and how to seek assistance.
14 CFR §120.115 / §120.225
21. Annual refresher training or education material is provided to all safety-sensitive employees.
14 CFR §120.115(a) | FAA Order 9790.1
22. All supervisors who can make reasonable suspicion determinations have received a minimum of 60 minutes of drug and 60 minutes of alcohol training covering the signs and symptoms of use.
14 CFR §120.115(b) / §120.225(b) | 49 CFR §40.61
★ CRITICAL — Supervisor training is mandatory; gaps here void the reasonable suspicion testing authority
23. A Substance Abuse Professional (SAP) evaluation process is in place for employees who violate the policy, including referral, evaluation, treatment/education requirements, and return-to-duty criteria.
49 CFR §§40.287–40.311 | 14 CFR §120.111
6
Records, Reporting & Self-Audit
Retention, annual MIS report, DAMIS registration
24. Test results and program records are retained per required periods: verified positive results and refusals for 5 years; annual MIS reports for 5 years; negative results for 1 year; alcohol test results ≥0.02 for 5 years.
49 CFR §40.333 | 14 CFR §120.131
25. Annual Management Information System (MIS) data is reported to the FAA Drug Abatement Division by March 15 of each year as required.
14 CFR §120.135 | FAA MIS reporting requirement
26. The organization is registered with the FAA Drug Abatement Division (DAMIS) as an employer with a drug and alcohol testing program.
14 CFR §120.107(a) | FAA DAMIS system
27. The program has been audited internally or by a third party within the last 12 months; findings have corrective actions.
FAA Order 9790.1 | Industry best practice
28. If using a consortium or third-party administrator (C/TPA), the organization maintains a written service agent agreement and has confirmed the C/TPA performs all required functions.
49 CFR §40.15 | 49 CFR Part 40 Subpart Q
29. Employees are informed that the employer retains the right to conduct reasonable suspicion testing and that refusal to test is treated as a positive result and program violation.
14 CFR §120.111 | 49 CFR §40.61
30. The DER and any supervisors involved in program administration have received training on their specific program responsibilities, including confidentiality requirements and the prohibition on disclosing test results except as authorized by 49 CFR Part 40.
49 CFR §40.333(f) | 49 CFR §40.321
