## 1. Purpose

Management review is where top management personally steps back from day-to-day AI operations and asks: *is our AI Management System still suitable, adequate, and effective — and is it still fit for the AI risk we're actually carrying?* This document gives [Organization Name] a ready-to-use agenda, an input checklist so nothing important gets left out, and a minutes template so decisions and actions are traceable.

## 2. Scope

Applies to the AIMS-level management review meeting(s) covering all AI systems, sites, and processes within the AIMS scope (Document 2). Frequency: at minimum [annually], recommended [quarterly or semi-annually] given the pace of change in AI regulation and technology; higher-risk AI portfolios should review more often.

## 3. Roles & Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Top Management / CEO or delegate | Chairs the review; owns final decisions; accountable for resourcing agreed actions |
| AIMS Manager | Prepares the input pack; facilitates the meeting; drafts and distributes minutes |
| Risk Owner(s) | Present AI risk register status and treatment progress |
| Data/AI System Owners | Present system-level performance, incidents, and KPI data |
| Compliance/Legal (if applicable) | Present regulatory horizon-scan (e.g., EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF alignment — Documents 22–23) |
| Minute-taker | Records discussion, decisions, and actions; distributes within [3] business days |

## 4. Procedure

1. AIMS Manager schedules the review and requests inputs (Section 5) from process/system owners at least [10] business days in advance.
2. AIMS Manager compiles the Management Review Pack (inputs consolidated into a single document/deck) and circulates it to attendees at least [3] business days before the meeting.
3. Meeting is held using the Agenda template (Section 6).
4. Decisions and actions are captured in real time using the Minutes template (Section 7).
5. Minutes are approved by the chair and distributed to all attendees and relevant process owners within [3] business days.
6. Actions are logged and tracked to closure (link to Document 20 where an action stems from a nonconformity, or to the AI Objectives tracker — Document 10 — where it's a planning action).
7. Status of actions from the *previous* review is always the first substantive agenda item at the *next* review — this is what makes review cumulative rather than a repeated blank-slate exercise.

## 5. Required Inputs Checklist (intent of Clause 9.3)

Management review only works if it looks backward and forward. At minimum, the pack must cover:

- [ ] Status of actions from previous management review(s)
- [ ] Changes in external and internal issues relevant to the AIMS (e.g., new regulation, new competitor AI capability, organizational changes, new AI use cases adopted)
- [ ] Changes in needs/expectations of interested parties (customers, regulators, employees, affected persons)
- [ ] AIMS performance information, including:
  - [ ] Nonconformities and corrective actions (from Document 20)
  - [ ] Monitoring, measurement, and KPI results (from Document 21)
  - [ ] Internal audit results (from Document 18)
  - [ ] AI system incidents, near-misses, or misuse/abuse reports
  - [ ] Achievement of AIMS objectives (from Document 10)
- [ ] Feedback from interested parties (complaints, audit findings from customers/regulators, survey results)
- [ ] Adequacy of resources (people, budget, tooling, compute, competence)
- [ ] Effectiveness of actions taken to address AI risks and opportunities (from Document 6/7 risk register)
- [ ] Opportunities for continual improvement
- [ ] Regulatory horizon: upcoming EU AI Act / NIST AI RMF or other applicable obligation deadlines and readiness status (Documents 22–23)
