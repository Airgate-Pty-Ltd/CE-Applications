## 1. Purpose

This document defines how [The Organization] sets, plans, resources, and monitors its AI
Management System (AIMS) objectives, and provides the working template for recording them. It
exists to satisfy the intent of ISO/IEC 42001 Clause 6.2, which calls for AI-related objectives
that are consistent with the AI Policy, measurable where practicable, monitored, communicated,
and updated as appropriate, together with plans describing what will be done, what resources are
required, who is responsible, when it will be completed, and how results will be evaluated.

In plain terms: the AI Policy states intentions; this document is where those intentions become
specific, dated commitments that someone owns and that top management checks on — the mechanism
that keeps "we care about responsible AI" from staying a slogan.

## 2. Scope

Applies to AIMS-level objectives set by top management and cascaded objectives set by AI System
Owners or function leads in support of the AIMS. Does not cover general business objectives
unrelated to AI governance.

## 3. Defined Terms

| Term | Working definition |
|---|---|
| Objective | A specific, intended outcome the AIMS is working toward, linked to the AI Policy. |
| Plan | The set of actions, resources, responsibilities, and timeframe needed to achieve an objective. |
| KPI / metric | A measurable indicator used to track progress toward an objective. |
| SMART | Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound — the quality bar objectives should meet wherever practicable. |

## 4. How Objectives Are Set

1. **Derive from policy.** Each objective must trace back to a specific commitment in the AI
   Policy (`01`, Section 5) or to a risk-treatment need identified in the AI Risk Register (`06`)
   or Risk Treatment Plan (`07`).
2. **Make them SMART where practicable.** Not every objective can be perfectly quantified (e.g.,
   "improve AI literacy across the organization"), but each should have at least one indicator
   that shows whether it is being achieved.
3. **Resource before committing.** An objective is not finalized until the resources
   (budget, people, time) needed to pursue it are identified and reasonably available — an
   unresourced objective is treated as a proposal, not a plan.
4. **Assign single-point ownership.** Every objective has one accountable owner, even if multiple
   people contribute to achieving it.
5. **Set a review cadence.** Progress is checked at a defined interval — at minimum, objectives
   are formally reviewed at each management review (`19`, when authored) and informally tracked
   more often for time-sensitive items.
6. **Update as needed.** Objectives are revised, closed, or replaced when circumstances change —
   an objective that is quietly abandoned without explanation is a finding waiting to happen in
   an audit.

## 5. Objective Categories (illustrative — tailor to organization)

- **Governance maturity** — e.g., completing AIMS documentation, training coverage.
- **Risk reduction** — e.g., closing High/Critical risk-treatment plans by a target date.
- **Fairness and impact** — e.g., completing bias testing across all customer-facing AI systems.
- **Transparency** — e.g., publishing model documentation/disclosures for in-scope systems.
- **Regulatory readiness** — e.g., achieving readiness against a specific upcoming regulatory
  deadline (e.g., an applicable EU AI Act obligation phase-in date — confirm current date before
  citing).
- **Operational reliability** — e.g., reducing model-drift-related incidents.

## 7. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Top management | Sets and approves top-level AIMS objectives; ensures resourcing; reviews progress at management review. |
| AIMS Manager | Coordinates objective-setting; consolidates the tracker; flags at-risk objectives. |
| Objective owners | Execute their assigned plan; report progress at the defined cadence. |
| Function leads | Provide resourcing and subject-matter support for objectives touching their area. |
