## 1. Purpose

This policy states [The Organization]'s commitment to the responsible development, provision,
and/or use of artificial intelligence (AI), and establishes the framework within which
[The Organization]'s AI Management System (AIMS) operates. It exists to satisfy the intent of
ISO/IEC 42001 Clause 5.2, which calls for top management to set a documented policy that fits
the organization's purpose and context, commits to meeting applicable requirements, and commits
to continually improving the AIMS.

In plain terms: this document is [The Organization]'s public and internal statement of *why*
it manages AI deliberately, *what* it promises to do (and not do), and *how* that promise turns
into day-to-day practice through the rest of the AIMS document set.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all AI systems that [The Organization] designs, develops, trains,
deploys, provides, procures, or otherwise uses in the course of its business, including:

- [AI System / Product Name 1 — brief description]
- [AI System / Product Name 2 — brief description]
- [Third-party or embedded AI components used in the organization's own products or operations]

It applies to all employees, contractors, and third parties who develop, operate, or make
decisions about these AI systems on [The Organization]'s behalf. The detailed boundary of what
is "in scope" for the AIMS is defined in `02-AIMS-Scope-and-Context-Statement.md`; this policy
is the governing statement of intent that sits above that boundary.

## 4. Policy Statement

[The Organization] is committed to managing artificial intelligence responsibly across its
entire lifecycle — from initial design or vendor selection through deployment, monitoring, and
eventual retirement. Top management has authorized this policy as the top-level expression of
that commitment, and has committed the resources, authority, and attention needed to make it
real rather than aspirational.

This policy commits [The Organization] to:

1. **Fit for purpose.** Aligning its AI governance approach to the actual scale, risk profile,
   and context of the AI systems it builds or uses — not a one-size-fits-all checklist.
2. **Meeting applicable requirements.** Satisfying the legal, regulatory, contractual, and
   voluntary commitments identified in the Interested Parties & Requirements Register.
3. **Continual improvement.** Treating the AIMS as a living system that is reviewed, tested
   against real incidents and near-misses, and improved — not a document written once and
   filed away.
4. **Being communicated and understood.** Making this policy available to all personnel and,
   on request, to relevant external interested parties, in language that is genuinely
   understandable rather than merely published.

## 5. Policy Principles / Commitments

[The Organization] commits to the following principles in all AI activity within scope of the AIMS:

1. **Human oversight and accountability.** AI systems that materially affect people or
   significant business decisions will have identified human owners and, where appropriate,
   human-in-the-loop or human-on-the-loop checkpoints. Accountability for an AI system's
   behavior never transfers to the AI system itself.
2. **Risk-based governance.** The rigor applied to any AI system — assessment, controls,
   monitoring frequency — scales with its potential impact, per the AI Risk Assessment
   Procedure (`06`) and AI System Impact Assessment Procedure (`09`).
3. **Legal and regulatory compliance.** [The Organization] will identify and meet its
   obligations under applicable AI-specific and general law (e.g., data protection, sector
   regulation, emerging AI-specific regulation such as the EU AI Act where applicable).
4. **Fairness and non-discrimination.** [The Organization] will take reasonable, documented
   steps to identify and mitigate unfair bias in AI system outcomes affecting individuals or
   groups.
5. **Transparency.** Where practical and not precluded by security, legal, or competitive
   constraints, [The Organization] will make clear to affected parties that AI is in use and
   provide meaningful information about its role in relevant decisions.
6. **Data governance.** Data used to train, fine-tune, evaluate, or operate AI systems will be
   managed for quality, provenance, and lawful basis of use, per the organization's data
   management practices.
7. **Security and robustness.** AI systems will be protected against misuse, adversarial
   manipulation, and unauthorized access, consistent with [The Organization]'s information
   security program.
8. **Third-party diligence.** AI systems or components sourced from vendors, partners, or
   open-source projects will be subject to proportionate due diligence before adoption.
9. **Resourcing.** Top management will allocate the people, budget, competence-building, and
   time needed to operate the AIMS effectively — this policy is a resourced commitment, not an
   unfunded mandate.
10. **Continual improvement.** Lessons from internal audits, incidents, near-misses, and
    management reviews will be fed back into this policy, its supporting procedures, and the
    risk register.
